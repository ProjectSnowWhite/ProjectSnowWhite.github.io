Updated 25 October 2026, 11:15 - transition tests: 20 and 10 frames, and a plain cut (first three entries). Work in progress: each entry says what changed and what is known to be wrong, so a known issue can be looked past.

  • Transition test - 20 frames of hand-over (2 seconds, test size)
    What it is: Flame salmon, 2 seconds at a quarter of the camera size (a test bench, so the picture is soft). Two pieces trained separately - one on 0 - 1.3 s, one on 0.7 - 2 s - and an in-between that hands over from the first to the second from frame 20 to frame 40 (0.67 - 1.33 s).
    New in it: The in-between: the first piece's splats start exactly as that piece left them and fade out by frame 40, the second piece's are invisible at frame 20 and end exactly as that piece starts; only the frames between are trained.
    Look at: Pause and step through 0.6 - 1.4 s (the timeline), and play it at normal speed: does anything double, flicker, swim or pop - the chef, the torch, the window behind the chef? Compare with the plain cut below, which pops at 1.0 s.
    Known issues: The picture is soft everywhere (test size). Where things move, the middle of the hand-over is a little weaker than either piece alone (a cross-fade, about frame 30). The in-between carries both pieces' splats, so this stretch is about 3 times the bytes - pairing the splats that are the same is the next step.
  • Transition test - 10 frames of hand-over (2 seconds, test size)
    What it is: The same two test pieces as the 20-frame test, handing over in a third of a second: from frame 25 to frame 35 (0.83 - 1.17 s).
    New in it: A shorter hand-over: the in-between has fewer free steps, so the first piece fades out and the second fades in faster.
    Look at: Step through 0.8 - 1.2 s and play at normal speed, against the 20-frame test and the plain cut: is a quick hand-over less visible than a slow one, or does it flicker?
    Known issues: Soft (test size). Its stretch is about 3 times the bytes too (both pieces' splats).
  • Transition test - the same two pieces with a plain cut at 1.0 s (reference)
    What it is: The same two test pieces as above, the first played to frame 30 and the second from frame 30 - nothing in between.
    New in it: Nothing: this is what two pieces trained separately look like when one simply follows the other.
    Look at: The jump at 1.0 s (frame 30): the window behind the chef and the chef change from one frame to the next. This is what the in-between above is meant to remove.
    Known issues: Soft (test size).
  • Flame salmon - the whole 10 seconds, newest: the later pieces as sharp as the first
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 9.3 Mbit/s, half the cameras' resolution, five 2-second pieces joined without a seam. The input videos are colour-corrected to the reference camera (the showcase footnote). Scores against the reference camera: 31.7 dB, LPIPS 0.132 (VGG) / 0.069 (Alex).
    New in it: The pieces after the first had too small a budget: two thirds of the first piece's training per frame, and almost no room for new splats (every splat seen at a join is carried on, so by the last piece only 4 % could be added). Now every piece trains as long per frame as the first, and can add as many splats as it carries. Where things move, pieces 3 and 4 are now as sharp as the first (28.8 / 28.9 dB against 28.3 before), the last piece +0.7 dB, the 8-second mark +0.9 dB; the whole clip 28.6 dB where things move against 28.2. It costs bytes: 9.3 Mbit/s against 6.0 - within the budget of 20.
    Look at: The man's hands and the torch in the second half (after the 4-second mark), and just after the 8-second mark. The room should look as before.
    Known issues: Pieces 2 and 5 are still about 0.4 dB under the first where things move. Viewed from away from the cameras, moving parts and objects close to the cameras can show a veil or a ghost (their depth is not pinned down well - being worked on; most visible in the spinach clip below).
  • Coffee martini - the whole 10 seconds, no ghost after the joins
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 6.2 Mbit/s, half the cameras' resolution, five 2-second pieces joined without a seam, the same way as the salmon above. The input videos are colour-corrected to the reference camera (the showcase footnote). Scores against the reference camera: 31.1 dB, LPIPS 0.140 (VGG) / 0.075 (Alex).
    New in it: The same fix as the salmon: the ghosting on moving parts just after each 2-second mark is gone. Where things move, the five frames after each mark are 0.9 - 1.9 dB better than in the previous version (29.4 / 28.6 / 28.5 / 28.8 dB against 28.5 / 27.4 / 27.0 / 26.9), and the whole clip is sharper and a little smaller (31.1 dB, LPIPS 0.075 at 6.2 Mbit/s against 31.0 / 0.077 at 6.3). The joins stay invisible where the scene stands still (0.02 - 0.05 grey levels).
    Look at: The man pouring and his hands just after the 2, 4, 6 and 8 second marks: they should move on smoothly, without a double image. The bottles, the shelves and the window should not change at the marks. The dog at the right edge.
    Known issues: The first half second is softer where things move (the fastest motion of the clip is there). The pieces in the middle lose about 0.3 dB in the last step before packing that the trained pieces did not have - being looked at. Moving hands remain the softest parts.
  • Cook spinach - the whole 10 seconds, seamless
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 5.3 Mbit/s, half the cameras' resolution, five 2-second pieces joined without a seam, made the same way as the salmon and the coffee above. The input videos are colour-corrected to the reference camera (the showcase footnote). Scores against the reference camera: 33.9 dB, LPIPS 0.140 (VGG) / 0.056 (Alex).
    New in it: A third scene with the same method: no seam and no ghost at the 2-second marks. Where the scene stands still, the change at a mark is the size of this scene's ordinary change from one frame to the next (0.11 - 0.17 grey levels at the marks, 0.10 - 0.14 inside the pieces).
    Look at: The cook's hands, the pan and the spinach just after the 2, 4, 6 and 8 second marks. The kitchen should not change at the marks.
    Known issues: The still kitchen moves a little more from frame to frame than in the salmon and the coffee (0.1 against 0.03 grey levels) - not yet looked into. The later pieces use the training budget that the salmon now shows was too small (being retrained with more).
  • Flame salmon - the whole 10 seconds (colour-corrected inputs, 22 October)
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 6.7 Mbit/s, half the cameras' resolution, five 2-second pieces with the still room held across them. The input videos are colour-corrected to the reference camera (the showcase footnote). Scores against the reference camera: 31.5 dB, LPIPS 0.140 (VGG) / 0.076 (Alex).
    New in it: Sharper fine texture than the 'still room' entry below: the training now also optimises for perceived detail, the spare splats go where several cameras agree the picture is wrong, the view through the windows starts from proper far-away points, and the finishing pass runs twice as long. Perceived error about 9 % lower (LPIPS 0.140 against 0.154), for 0.5 dB less on the pixel-average score - that score prefers a slightly softer picture.
    Look at: Fine texture: the salmon's fibres, the specks in the pan, labels on the shelves, the shirt. The view through the windows. The room at the 2, 4, 6 and 8 second marks (it should stay calm).
    Known issues: Fixed today: the whole-picture flash every 2 seconds was the player (for a frame or two it drew the old and the new piece on top of each other); it now prepares the next piece off-screen and fades over in 0.16 s. The first version of that fix waited too long for the next piece and showed one still frame from 2 seconds on; fixed, and the player now has tests for both. Still known: a faint shift of edges in the room at the hand-over (the pieces are trained apart; a training-side fix is next). Pieces 2 to 5 a little softer than the first 2 seconds. Moving hands and the flame remain the softest parts.
  • Flame salmon - the whole 10 seconds
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 5.5 Mbit/s (the public page's stream uses about 20). Trained at half the cameras' resolution, as five pieces of 2 seconds each, one after the other.
    New in it: Still things are now really still: within each 2-second piece the room no longer shimmers or wobbles. About a quarter of the public stream's bit-rate for a slightly better picture by the numbers.
    Look at: The room standing quiet while the person moves; the hands and the torch flame (the hardest parts); detail on the shelves; the trees through the windows.
    Known issues: Every 2 seconds the still room jumps slightly, where one piece hands over to the next - a fix is being tested. The last 2 seconds are a little softer (that piece stopped training too early; fixed for later runs).
  • Flame salmon - the whole 10 seconds, the room held still across the pieces
    What it is: The same 10 seconds as the first entry, but each 2-second piece keeps the still room of the piece before instead of building its own. About 4.9 Mbit/s (11 % less than the first entry).
    New in it: The jump every 2 seconds is gone: where one piece hands over to the next, the still room now changes no more than the camera's own noise does between two frames (before: about four times that - the visible pop).
    Look at: Watch the walls, the shelves and the window frames at the 2, 4, 6 and 8 second marks - compare with the first entry, where the room jumps there. Then the person and the flame.
    Known issues: Pieces 2 to 5 are slightly softer than in the first entry (the room they keep cannot follow small changes of light; about 0.75 dB) - the next step lets it change a little. The first hand-over (at 2 s) still shows a small jump.
  • Flame salmon - the first 2 seconds, sharpest
    What it is: The first 2 seconds of the clip above, with one extra finishing pass that optimises for perceived detail (what the eye judges, not only the average pixel error).
    New in it: Sharper texture for the same bit-rate - the perceptual error is about 8 % lower than in the same 2 seconds of the 10-second clip.
    Look at: Compare fine texture with the first 2 seconds of the 10-second clip: labels and items on the shelves, the shirt, the face, the pan and the food.
    Known issues: Only 2 seconds long (it plays round). Same half resolution as above.
  • Flame salmon - the first 2 seconds at full camera resolution
    What it is: The same first 2 seconds, trained on the cameras' full-size pictures (2690 x 2017: four times the pixels of the entries above), with the detail-finishing pass. About 9 Mbit/s; about a third more splats than the half-size version.
    New in it: Your question: does training at full resolution show more? By our measurements, at normal viewing size it is about as sharp as the half-size version - we found the trainer leaves most of its splats unused, which is now the limit (being worked on). Zooming in is where full resolution should show.
    Look at: Zoom in on fine texture - labels and items on the shelves, the shirt's fabric, the food, the face - and compare with the '2 seconds, sharpest' entry at the same zoom.
    Known issues: The person's hands and the torch flame are softer than the room (moving parts are the hardest; being worked on). Only 2 seconds long (it plays round).
