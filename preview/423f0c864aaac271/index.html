Updated 21 October 2026, 11:00. Work in progress: each entry says what changed and what is known to be wrong, so a known issue can be looked past.

  • Flame salmon - the whole 10 seconds
    What it is: The full 300-frame clip as one stream of 5.5 Mbit/s (the public page's stream uses about 20). Trained at half the cameras' resolution, as five pieces of 2 seconds each, one after the other.
    New in it: Still things are now really still: within each 2-second piece the room no longer shimmers or wobbles. About a quarter of the public stream's bit-rate for a slightly better picture by the numbers.
    Look at: The room standing quiet while the person moves; the hands and the torch flame (the hardest parts); detail on the shelves; the trees through the windows.
    Known issues: Every 2 seconds the still room jumps slightly, where one piece hands over to the next - a fix is being tested. The last 2 seconds are a little softer (that piece stopped training too early; fixed for later runs).
  • Flame salmon - the first 2 seconds, sharpest
    What it is: The first 2 seconds of the clip above, with one extra finishing pass that optimises for perceived detail (what the eye judges, not only the average pixel error).
    New in it: Sharper texture for the same bit-rate - the perceptual error is about 8 % lower than in the same 2 seconds of the 10-second clip.
    Look at: Compare fine texture with the first 2 seconds of the 10-second clip: labels and items on the shelves, the shirt, the face, the pan and the food.
    Known issues: Only 2 seconds long (it plays round). Same half resolution as above.
  • Flame salmon - the first 2 seconds at full camera resolution
    What it is: The same first 2 seconds, trained on the cameras' full-size pictures (2690 x 2017: four times the pixels of the entries above), with the detail-finishing pass. About 9 Mbit/s; about a third more splats than the half-size version.
    New in it: Your question: does training at full resolution show more? By our measurements, at normal viewing size it is about as sharp as the half-size version - we found the trainer leaves most of its splats unused, which is now the limit (being worked on). Zooming in is where full resolution should show.
    Look at: Zoom in on fine texture - labels and items on the shelves, the shirt's fabric, the food, the face - and compare with the '2 seconds, sharpest' entry at the same zoom.
    Known issues: The person's hands and the torch flame are softer than the room (moving parts are the hardest; being worked on). Only 2 seconds long (it plays round).
